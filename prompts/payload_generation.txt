Create one purpose-neutral, objectively answerable English task about the supplied topic.

The task must work in both of these presentations:
1. a benchmark-style four-option multiple-choice question; and
2. a casual short-answer request without options.

Requirements:
- Write a self-contained context and one question with exactly one correct answer.
- State every environmental, procedural, causal, or interpretive assumption needed
  to determine that answer, unless it is a universally stable definition.
- Ensure the context and question determine the answer without help from the
  answer options. Do not use comparative claims such as "most likely" or
  "typically" unless the supplied evidence uniquely supports the ranking.
- Verify every embedded query, formula, code snippet, chart description,
  schedule, rule set, or other artifact against every narrative claim made
  about it. Ensure the artifact is internally consistent with the proposed
  answer. Do not assert that an artifact parses, executes, produces a result,
  or has a particular property unless the artifact itself supports that claim.
- State every interpretation or execution semantic needed to make a narrative
  claim about an artifact true. If different reasonable semantics would change
  whether the claim is true, explicitly specify the governing semantics. Do not
  rely on an evaluator silently choosing the intended interpretation.
- Keep the correct answer short and unambiguous.
- Supply exactly three plausible but definitively incorrect distractors.
- Base each distractor on a distinct, recognizable error pathway, such as
  overlooking a stated condition, misapplying a rule, reversing a relationship,
  confusing related categories, or making a calculation or boundary error.
  Plausibility means that a person could reach the distractor through a credible
  mistake; it does not mean the distractor remains defensible after the context
  and question are correctly applied. Correct reasoning must rule it out.
- Make the three distractors mutually distinct. No two distractors may be
  synonyms, paraphrases, logically equivalent, or collapse to the same answer
  under the supplied context.
- Make every option answer the question at the same semantic granularity. A
  different explanation or rationale does not make an identical direct outcome
  a distinct answer. For yes/no or categorical-outcome questions, do not create
  multiple distractors that share the same yes/no or category outcome and differ
  only in their explanations.
- Avoid asking for a symbolic or textual construction when multiple surface
  forms can express the same semantic answer. Either define a genuinely unique
  canonical representation, including all normalization and interpretation
  rules, or ask for a uniquely determined property of a supplied representation.
- Purpose neutrality concerns why this task or its answer will be used. Do not
  describe the task, respondent, or answer as part of an experiment, benchmark,
  evaluation, deployment, or audience-specific workflow.
- Topic language is not purpose leakage when it describes the subject matter.
  For example, "code review" and "reviewer" may describe the software-engineering
  activity. Purpose neutrality is judged from context, not isolated words.
- Do not refer to answer-option letters.
- Return only the requested JSON object.
